## definition
Rewriting your commits so they start from a newer point on another branch. Git does not move
your commits — it makes new ones with the same changes, one at a time, on top of the other
branch. The result is a straight line of history rather than a join, which is easier to read
later. Because the new commits are not the same commits, this is only safe while they are
still only on your computer.
